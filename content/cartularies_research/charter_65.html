Source: Eynsham Cartulary, H.E, Salter (ed.)

Charter: 65

Title:De terra Oxenefordia

Approximate date: c1130 - 1135

Original Latin

Robertus de Oilio omnibus baronibus suis & burgensibus de Oxenefordia salutem. Sciatis quod ego concedo Rualdo clerico terram que fuit Lefsi & illam terram quam emit de Sabod & terram Rogerii Scuelai quietam & liberam de omni seruitio & de omni redditu, eo dante per unumquemque annum vi denarios ad Natiuitatem domini ad oblationem meam & uxoris mee. Et concedo ut has predictas det post obitum suum cui placuerit ita quietas & liberas sicut predixi. Teste Roberto filio Regis, & Edida uxore mea, & Fulcone fratre meo, & Roberto filio Widonis, Roberto filio Petri, Ricardo de Alnod, Ricardo de Brai, Willelmo filio Isward, Rogero de Tiwia.

Translation

Robert de Oilio sends greetings to all his barons and burgesses of Oxford. Know that I grant to Ruald the clerk the land that formerly belonged to Lefsi, together with the land that he purchased from Sabod, and the land of Roger Scuelai, to be held free and quit from all service and from every rent. In return, he shall give each year six pence at the Feast of the Nativity of Our Lord (Christmas) as an offering on behalf of myself and my wife. I also grant that after his death he may give these aforesaid lands to whomever he pleases, to hold them just as freely and without obligation as I have described. Witnesses: Robert son of Reg[is], Edith my wife, Fulk my brother, Robert son of Wido, Robert son of Peter, Richard de Alnod, Richard de Brai, William son of Isward, and Roger de Tiwia.

Notes on Language

baronibus suis

literally:

"his barons." In the twelfth century this referred to Robert's principal men or vassals, not necessarily hereditary barons in the later peerage sense.

clerico

literally:

a clerk or cleric. Ruald was a man in clerical orders or employed in a clerical capacity.

quietam & liberam

literally:

"free and quit." This is a standard legal expression meaning the land was held free from the specified obligations.

de omni servitio & de omni redditu

literally:

"from all service and from every rent." The grant exempted the land from normal feudal services and rents, except for the annual payment specified.

vi denarios

literally:

six denarii (six pence), paid annually at Christmas as a symbolic acknowledgement of the grant.

ut ... det post obitum suum cui placuerit

literally:

Robert grants Ruald the right to dispose of the land after his death to whomever he chooses. This is an unusually generous provision and may reflect the fact that the grantee was a cleric.

Nature of Transaction Grant of landholding rights / confirmation of tenure Historical Significance Charter 65 is already giving us something Charter 64 did not: It shows Robert D'Oyly exercising lordship over land and tenants in Oxford itself.

important evidence here is:

  • Robert addresses the burgesses of Oxford.
  • Robert grants rights to Ruald.
  • The land consists of multiple holdings.
The arrangement includes a continuing annual payment. This is a very different view of Robert from Charter 64, where he appeared primarily as a benefactor of Eynsham Abbey. Here we see Robert as a landholder and administrator.
