Source: Eynsham Cartulary, H. E. Salter (ed.)

Charter: 64

Title: Carta Roberti de Oili de terra in Oxenefordia

Approximate date: c.1130–1135

Original Latin

Notum sit omnibus fidelibus tam presentibus quam futuris, quod Robertus de Oileio & uxor eius & filii dederunt deo & ecclesie sancte Marie de Eynesham illam terram de feodo suo que est in Mullesford, & totam illam terram quam tenuit de eo Rualdus in Oxenefordia, pro anima regis & pro animabus suis & pro animabus patrum suorum & matrum, solidam & quietam sicut elemosinam. Et ad hoc confirmandum dote ecclesiastica super altare miserunt. Huius donationis testes sunt, Robertus filius Widonis, Radulfus filius Rogeri, & Hugo frater eius, & Nigellus frater ipsius Roberti, & Adeliza filia Reginaldi, Ketel & Lefwinus Canc, Nicholaus filius Sawoldi, & Robertus filius Osberti, Rannulfus.

Translation

Let it be known to all the faithful, both present and future, that Robert de Oileio and his wife and sons gave to God and the church of Saint Mary of Eynsham that land of his fee which is at Mullesford, and all that land which Rualdus held from him in Oxford, for the soul of the king and for their own souls and for the souls of their fathers and mothers, firm and free as alms. And to confirm this donation they placed an ecclesiastical offering upon the altar.
The witnesses of this donation are:
Robert son of Wido, Ralph son of Roger, and Hugh his brother, and Nigel the brother of that Robert, Adeliza daughter of Reginald, Ketel and Lefwin the Chancellor, Nicholas son of Sawold, Robert son of Osbert, and Ranulf.

Notes on Language

in perpetuam elemosinam

Literally:

"in perpetual alms." A standard legal formula meaning the land was granted permanently to the Church as a pious gift and was generally exempt from ordinary feudal obligations. quietam et liberam "Free and quit." Indicates that the land was to be held without interference and with the specified exemptions.

Notum sit

Literally:

"Let it be known..." A formal announcement.

omnibus fidelibus

Literally:

"to all the faithful" This does not simply mean "Christians." It is effectively addressing everyone who has a legal or moral interest in the transaction.

Historical Notes

The document is:
  • a formal charter, intended to have permanent legal force, written to preserve rights for future generations, concerning Robert D'Oyly.
From this charter we can state:
  • Robert de Oili is acting as the grantor.
  • The recipient is the Church of St Mary of Eynsham.
  • Directly Stated Robert de Oili, his wife and sons make the grant.
  • The recipient is St Mary's, Eynsham.
  • The property consists of land at Mullesford and land in Oxford held by Ruald.
  • The grant is made for the souls of the king, the donors and their ancestors.
  • The donation is confirmed by placing the gift upon the altar.
  • Witnesses are listed.

Historical Interpretation

  • This appears to be a pious grant intended to secure perpetual ownership by the Abbey.
  • The participation of Robert's wife and sons suggests family agreement regarding the transfer.
  • The ceremony at the altar indicates a formal ecclesiastical confirmation of the gift.
  • The grant forms part of the early relationship between the D'Oyly family and Eynsham Abbey.

Historical Significance

Primary significance

This charter records an early grant of land by Robert de Oili, together with his wife and sons, to St Mary's Abbey, Eynsham.
It establishes the D'Oyly family's direct involvement with the Abbey during the early twelfth century.

Wider historical context

The charter belongs to the period shortly after the Norman settlement of Oxfordshire, when the D'Oyly family were among the principal Norman landholders in the county.

Importance for the D'Oyly study

This charter provides:

  • one of the earliest documentary references in this section of the Cartulary;
  • evidence of family participation in the donation;
  • evidence of landholding in both Mullesford and Oxford;
  • the beginning of the documented relationship between the D'Oyly family and Eynsham Abbey.
