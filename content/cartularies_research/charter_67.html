Source: Eynsham Cartulary, H. E. Salter (ed.)

Charter: 67

Title: De Anschetillo de Cleindona.

Translation of Title: Concerning Anschetill of Cleydon.

Approximate Date: Before the death of Robert d'Oilly in 1142 (editorial note).

Original Latin

Notum sit omnibus tam futuris quam presentibus quod ego Edid uxor Roberti de Oilio dedi & concessi monachis ecclesie Egneshamie hominem quendam de terra mea de Cleindona, Anschetillum nomine, cum uxore sua & filiis & omni catallo suo in perpetuam elemosinam.

Translation

Let it be known to all, present and future, that I, Edith, wife of Robert de Oili, have given and granted to the monks of the church of Saint Mary of Eynsham Anschetill of Claydon, together with his wife, his children, and all his possessions, to be held in perpetual alms.

Notes on Language

Ego Edid uxor Roberti de Oilio

Literally

"I, Edith, wife of Robert d'Oilly." This is the first charter in this series issued solely in Edith's name.

terra mea de Cleindona

Literally

"My land at Cleydon." The wording indicates that Edith regarded the estate at Cleydon as her own property or one over which she exercised recognised rights. The charter itself does not explain the legal basis of those rights.

hominem quendam

literally

"A certain man." The grant concerns a named individual rather than a parcel of land.

cum uxore sua et filiis

Literally

"With his wife and children." The grant includes Anschetill's immediate family.

cum omni catallo suo

Literally

"With all his chattels." Catallum refers to movable property or possessions. The charter transfers nschetill together with his family and movable goods.

in perpetuam elemosinam

Literally

"In perpetual alms." The standard legal formula indicating that the gift was intended to be permanent and made as a pious donation to the Church.

Historical Notes

Directly stated by the charter Edith, wife of Robert d'Oilly, is the grantor. She grants Anschetill of Cleydon. The grant includes Anschetill's wife, children and all his chattels. The gift is made to the monks of Eynsham Abbey. The grant is made in perpetual alms.

Editorial Evidence (Salter)

Salter notes that the charter was made before the death of Robert d'Oilly in 1142 and identifies Claydon, Buckinghamshire, as the dowry of Edith Forne, Robert's wife.

Historical Interpretation

The charter is unusual because Edith acts independently rather than jointly with Robert. The wording "my land at Cleydon" is consistent with Salter's editorial suggestion that Claydon formed part of Edith's marriage property.

However, this explanation comes from the editorial note rather than the charter itself.

Unlike the previous charters, this document records the transfer of a dependent man and his household rather than a grant of land.

    Historical Significance

    This charter is the first in the present series to be issued solely in Edith's name.

    It demonstrates that Edith exercised recognised authority in relation to property associated with Cleydon.

    The charter is also significant because it records the transfer of a dependent tenant and his household rather than the transfer of land itself.

    It therefore provides evidence for another aspect of estate administration within the D'Oilly lordship.

    When read alongside Salter's editorial note, the charter suggests that Claydon held a particular significance within Edith's own property interests, although that conclusion depends upon evidence external to the charter.
